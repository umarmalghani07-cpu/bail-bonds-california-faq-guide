# California Bail Bonds — Legal Glossary

Definitions for 25 key terms used across all guides in this repository.
All definitions reflect California law and practice as of 2026.

---

## A

### Arraignment
The first court appearance after arrest, during which the defendant is formally
informed of the charges and asked to enter a plea. Under California Penal Code
§ 825, arraignment must occur within 48 hours of arrest (excluding Sundays and
holidays). The judge sets or reviews bail at arraignment.

---

## B

### Bail
Money or property deposited with a court to secure a defendant's release from
custody and guarantee their appearance at all future court proceedings. If the
defendant appears as required, bail is returned at the end of the case.
Defined under California Penal Code § 1268.

### Bail Agent
A licensed professional authorized by the California Department of Insurance
to execute surety bonds on behalf of an insurance company. Bail agents charge
a non-refundable premium (typically 10% of the bail amount) in exchange for
posting the full bail with the court. Also called a bail bondsman.

### Bail Forfeiture
The legal process by which a court declares bail lost when a defendant fails
to appear. Under California Penal Code § 1305, the court declares a forfeiture
and the bail agent has 185 days to return the defendant to custody or vacate
the forfeiture before a summary judgment is entered.

### Bail Hearing
A court proceeding at which a judge determines whether to grant bail and at
what amount. The judge considers the defendant's flight risk, ties to the
community, criminal history, and — following In re Humphrey (2021) —
the defendant's ability to pay.

### Bail Schedule
A chart of preset bail amounts for specific offenses, adopted by each county's
superior court and the California Judicial Council. Allows bail to be posted
immediately after booking without a judge's appearance, using the scheduled
amount for the charge.

### Bench Warrant
A warrant issued directly by a judge (from the bench) ordering the arrest of
a defendant who failed to appear for a scheduled court date. Issuance of a
bench warrant triggers bail forfeiture proceedings under PC § 1305.

### Booking
The administrative process that occurs at a jail after arrest. Includes
fingerprinting, photographing, recording personal information, and entering
charges into the system. Bail can typically be posted after booking is complete.

### Bond
See *Surety Bond*. In common usage, "bond" refers to the bail bond posted by
a licensed bail agent on behalf of a defendant. Legally distinct from "bail,"
which refers to the total security amount set by the court.

---

## C

### Cash Bail
Payment of the full bail amount in cash directly to the court or jail.
If the defendant appears at all required hearings, cash bail is fully refunded
at the end of the case (minus administrative fees). No ongoing premium is
charged, but the full amount is tied up for the duration of the case.

### Co-Signer
A person who signs the bail bond agreement alongside the defendant, accepting
personal financial liability if the defendant fails to appear. The co-signer
(also called an indemnitor) may be required to pledge collateral and is
responsible for helping locate the defendant if they skip bail.

### Collateral
Property or assets pledged as additional security for a bail bond, beyond the
premium paid. Common forms include real estate equity, vehicles, jewelry, and
bank accounts. Collateral is returned when the case concludes and bail is
exonerated, provided the defendant appeared at all hearings.

---

## D

### Delivery Bond
An immigration bond that secures the release of a person detained by ICE
(U.S. Immigration & Customs Enforcement). The bonded person must appear at
all immigration court hearings. Issued through EOIR (Executive Office for
Immigration Review), not a state criminal court.

---

## E

### Exoneration
The legal discharge of a bail bond by the court, releasing the bail agent and
any co-signers from further liability. Exoneration typically occurs when the
criminal case concludes (by conviction, acquittal, or dismissal) and the
defendant has appeared at all required hearings.

---

## F

### Failure to Appear (FTA)
A defendant's non-appearance at a required court date without prior court
approval. Results in a bench warrant, bail forfeiture, and potentially an
additional criminal charge under California Penal Code § 1320.

### Flight Risk
A judicial assessment that a defendant is likely to flee the jurisdiction to
avoid prosecution. Courts consider ties to the community, criminal history,
prior failures to appear, severity of charges, and available resources when
assessing flight risk. High flight risk typically results in higher bail or
denial of bail.

---

## I

### Indemnitor
See *Co-Signer*. The formal legal term for the person who indemnifies (agrees
to compensate) the bail agent for any losses if the defendant fails to appear.
The indemnitor signs the bail bond application and bears financial
responsibility for the full bond amount if bail is forfeited.

---

## O

### Own Recognizance (OR) Release
Release from custody based solely on the defendant's written promise to appear
at all future court dates, without requiring money bail. Granted by a judge
when the defendant poses minimal flight risk and public safety risk.
Governed by California Penal Code § 1318.

---

## P

### Premium
The non-refundable fee charged by a bail agent for posting a surety bond.
California law sets the standard premium at 10% of the total bail amount.
The premium is earned by the bail agent regardless of the case outcome and
is not returned even if charges are dropped.

### Pretrial Release
Any form of release from custody before trial, including OR release, cash
bail, surety bond, and property bond. California law favors the least
restrictive pretrial release conditions that reasonably assure appearance.

### Property Bond
A bail bond secured by real estate instead of cash or a commercial surety.
The property owner records a lien on real property in favor of the court.
The court may foreclose on the property if the defendant fails to appear.
Requires a court assessment of property value and equity.

---

## R

### Remand
A court order returning a defendant to custody, either by revoking previously
granted bail or by ordering detention after a bail hearing. A defendant may be
remanded if they violate bail conditions, are deemed a public safety risk,
or fail to appear.

---

## S

### Surety Bond
A three-party agreement in which a bail agent (the surety) guarantees to the
court that a defendant will appear at all required hearings. The three parties
are: the court (obligee), the defendant (principal), and the bail agent (surety).
The surety company backs the bond and pays the full amount if the defendant
fails to appear and is not returned within the 185-day forfeiture period.

---

## V

### Voluntary Departure Bond
An immigration bond posted when a person agrees to voluntarily leave the
United States by a specific date, in lieu of formal deportation proceedings.
If the person leaves by the required date, the bond is returned. If they
remain past the deadline, the bond is forfeited.
